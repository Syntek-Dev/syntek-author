## [0.3.0] - 04/10/2026

Every section's ledger entry now records how the section moved from its original to its final text, revision by revision and with who made each change, and `make compare` prints that record as a PDF (D45).

### Added

- **The three-stage comparison, in every variant (D45).** `make compare` writes `build/compare/<unit>.pdf` for each unit with ledger entries. `UNIT=<unit>` (the brief's filename stem, or a path ending in it) narrows it to one unit, and `SECTION=<slug>` to one section of it, written to `build/compare/<unit>--<section>.pdf`. Each PDF opens with a cover holding the key, the unit's disclosure row exactly as `make provenance` prints it, with its note, and a table of its sections (origin, stages recorded, revisions, change ratio, promoted). Each section follows in the order of the brief's `sections:` list, with a heading block (its revision list and two change figures), a portrait **redline** (the final text, every added word marked and every removed word struck where it stood, by whoever made the change) and a landscape page in **three columns** (Original, AI edit, Final, aligned by paragraph, the headings repeated on every page the columns run on to; the AI edit is the state after the last `ai` or `author-note` revision). A section reopened since its promotion is shown to its latest revision and labelled reopened, never with its previous final drawn as a later state. Each actor has an Okabe–Ito colour and a line style of its own, so the page reads in greyscale and to colour-blind readers: the AI blue, dashed underline and cross-out; the author's note green, dotted underline and double strike; the author vermillion, solid underline and single strike; a change in an entry written before the record grey, wavy underline and wavy strike, shown but never assigned. A citation, code or maths takes its actor's line around a box, link text takes its actor's colour (unchanged links print black), a short word always carries its dash or dot, and a long marked word or web address can break, so nothing runs off the page. `FORMAT=tex` stops at the LaTeX (`build/compare/<unit>/<unit>.tex`) and needs Pandoc alone; the PDF runs XeLaTeX twice and reports every character a font could not draw. With no entries the target prints 'No sections to compare yet.' and exits `0`; `SECTION=` without `UNIT=` is refused (exit `2`); a malformed entry is left out, listed on the cover, and fails the run once the rest is written; a unit Pandoc or XeLaTeX cannot set is named, the other units are still written, and the run fails. The output is for the author and for disclosure, and is never issued, committed or synced.
- **`tooling/compare.py`** (standard library, Python 3.11 or later) with `units`, `ast --unit U [--section S] … -o FILE` (the comparison as Pandoc JSON) and `--self-test` (no TeX needed; its Pandoc cases run only where Pandoc exists). It reads the ledger through `provenance.py`, so it never reads an entry or brief git ignores (D42). Each state goes through Pandoc and the house filter, so emphasis, quotation marks, links, citations and footnotes are kept and comments dropped. Each step of the chain is compared word by word, as `provenance.py` compares, and no stage an entry does not hold is guessed. **`tooling/compare.yaml`** holds its Pandoc defaults (article class, A4, 10 pt, 18 mm margins), read after `tooling/defaults.yaml`. **`tooling/latex/compare.tex`** is its header include: `xcolor`, `ulem`, `paracol` and `array`; the `\cmp…` macros, which cannot clash with a house macro and never enter a document, so `texcheck.py` never learns them; the key; and a true landscape page, because `pdflscape`'s rotation never reaches the pages `paracol` ships, after which the next portrait page gets its full height back.
- **The revision record (D45, D12).** An entry written from v0.3.0 carries `format: 2` and two optional sections between `## AI original` and `## Author final`. `## Author original` holds the text the loop started from when the AI did not draft it: the first skill to revise an author-drafted section writes it, and it stays empty in an AI-drafted entry. `## Revisions` is the ordered chain of full-text states, oldest first, each opened by one marker, `<!-- revision N · <kind> · DD/MM/YYYY · <skill> (<strength>) · rows a–b -->`. The kind names who made the change, in D38's words: `ai` (an AI suggestion the author accepted), `author-note` (the AI applying the author's own note or flag answer) or `author` (the author's own edits, found by comparing the draft with the last recorded state before anything changes). Whatever differs between the last revision and the Author final is the author's, and a state equal to the one before it is never written. `standards/style/ledger/CONTEXT.md` defines the format, and `CLAUDE.md` the procedure: before a change, after it, at a redraft, at promotion and for a correction after promotion.
- **`provenance.py` reads the record.** Its entries gain `format`, `author_original`, `revisions`, `original` and `chain()`. `check` reports a malformed or out-of-sequence marker, an unknown kind, a date the calendar lacks, a revision with no text, Author original text in an AI-drafted entry, revisions without an Author original in an author-drafted one, a misplaced or repeated heading, a marker under any other heading and a `format` other than `2`; it warns on a revision identical to the state before it, word for word and paragraph for paragraph, so a paragraph break added or removed is a change, and an Author final that differs only in its paragraphs is the author's step. `Entry.reopened` says when a promoted section has changed since and not been promoted again (a revision records its final as `promoted <date>`, or is dated after the promotion); `chain()` then ends at the last revision, because the Author final is the previous one. `disclosure_row()` and the table's headings and note are importable, so `make compare`'s cover prints the same row, and `make provenance` prints exactly what it printed before. An optional heading counts only where the record puts it, so a document's own `## Revisions` part stays in its text. The change ratio is unchanged (AI original against Author final), and an entry without `format: 2` reads exactly as before.
- **The worked example shows all three stages.** The example opening's entry carries an invented `## Author original`, one recorded `improve-section (light)` revision and two decision rows, one accepted and one rejected with the author's reason. So `make compare UNIT=01-example-chapter` shows original, AI edit and final on a new project, and `learn-voice` has a rejection to practise on (Section 3.2). The-turn's and the business example's entries carry `format: 2` with both optional sections empty. Seed-once examples reach new projects only.
- **Audits.** `tooling-smoke.sh` checks 33–37, in every render. A ledger entry planted with a multi-round chain (an `ai`, an `author` and an `author-note` revision, then the author's final) and a numbered list and a quotation in every state gives `make compare FORMAT=tex` LaTeX carrying each actor's macro (33). An entry git ignores beside it never reaches `build/compare/` or the log, while the tracked entry's words do (34, D42). `SECTION=` without `UNIT=` is refused before any output (35). An empty ledger prints 'No sections to compare yet.' and exits `0` (36). The PDF builds where XeLaTeX exists, and is a named skip elsewhere, CI included (37). Check 18 runs `compare.py --self-test` with the other tooling self-tests; it proves, without TeX, that every paragraph shape the redline opens is defined in `compare.tex`. `_common.sh` catalogues `tooling/compare.py`, `tooling/compare.yaml` and `tooling/latex/compare.tex` as shipped to every variant.

### Changed

- **Every skill that changes a draft's words records a revision (D45).**
  - `draft-section` writes `format: 2` and the empty optional sections. On a redraft it first records the author's own work in the old draft (and a promoted section's final), then moves the old original and every revision into the dated `<!-- superseded original, DD/MM/YYYY: … -->` comment (each inner `-->` written `--&gt;`), sets `drafted` to the day of the redraft, and the chain starts again. A promoted section's final goes with its chain, so its Author final, `promoted` and `change_ratio` are emptied and its `provenance.md` row removed: it is a draft again until it is promoted. Its own proofreading pass records nothing, because the entry records its result.
  - `improve-section` writes the Author original when it creates the entry, records the author's hand-edits as an `author` revision before proposing anything, and appends one `ai` revision, `improve-section (<strength>)` with the pass's rows, once the author has accepted something.
  - `adapt-section` compares the draft with the last recorded state (no longer 'the last AI pass') and appends an `author-note` revision, then an `ai` one, when a run makes both kinds of change. Text the AI writes from a source is an AI original; in business, template adaptation and ingest write the Author original.
  - `promote-section` records a flag answer that changes the words as an `author-note` revision. On a re-promotion it checks that the previous Author final is already in the chain (the first skill to change a reopened section appends it, its skill field `promoted DD/MM/YYYY`) before it writes the new one.
  - `spelling`, `grammar`, `tone` (business) and `fact-check` bring a `format: 2` entry level with the draft, then append one `ai` revision naming the skill and the decision rows just logged (a proofread is one revision, named `spelling`). An entry without `format: 2` gets its rows only, and placing or removing a flag is not a revision. Run as a step of another skill's pass over the AI's own text (`draft-section` and the draft workflows' proofreading step, `adapt-section` step 8), `spelling` and `grammar` leave the recording to that skill, so the AI's own text is never credited to the author.
  - In business, `promote-section` and `library/workflows/04-promote-a-section` stop on a decision row still open: a holding line is the AI's wording kept only until the author chooses, so it is never promoted as accepted.
- **A correction after promotion keeps the record (D45).** `standards/style/ledger/CLAUDE.md` holds the one procedure, which both review workflows (`manuscript/workflows/05-review-a-chapter`, `library/workflows/05-review-a-document`) follow: the previous Author final goes into the chain as an `author` revision unless the last state already equals it, then the section as found if the author changed it since the last recorded state, then the correction as its own revision; then the new Author final, `learned: false`, a recomputed change ratio, the section's row in `provenance.md`, and `provenance.py check`. Comparing with the last state, not any earlier one, keeps an author's revert before promotion on record as the author's.
- **`learn-voice` mines the chain (D45, D38).** The author's evidence is each `author` revision, the gap from the last revision to the final and, for an author-drafted section, the whole Author original. Words an `ai` or `author-note` revision brought in never count as the author's, even once accepted. An AI change the author later undoes counts as an implicit rejection, and each rejection is read against the text it was proposed for. An entry mined and then given a new final is read only after its last `promoted …` revision; one reopened and not yet promoted again lends only its decisions and stays unlearned, because its final is still to come; and an entry without `format: 2` is read as before, as weaker evidence. In business an Author original copied from a template, or from a document someone else wrote, is not the author's voice. Both `07-learn-from-your-edits` workflows follow.
- **The rules, guides and loop workflows describe the record.** `.claude/rules/syntek-author/03-authorship.md` routes provenance to the ledger's `CONTEXT.md` and `CLAUDE.md` and names `make compare`, and both `drafting-with-ai.md` guides mention it. Workflows 01–04 in both content layers, and the business ingest workflow (08), write the record as their skills do.
- **The build (D45).** `.claude/rules/syntek-author/04-build-pipeline.md` gains the comparison's pipeline, its targets row, `UNIT=` and `SECTION=`, and what it needs (Pandoc, and XeLaTeX with `xcolor`, `ulem`, `paracol` and `array`); the `tooling/` and `tooling/latex/` pairs list the new files. `make compare`'s `FORMAT` counts only when given on the `make` command line, so one the shell exports changes nothing.
- **`README.md`** gives `make compare`'s requirements, extends the Provenance bullet and the `tooling-smoke.sh` row, and tells a project updating from v0.2.0 or earlier what to expect. The generated `README.md` names `make compare` and its TeX packages, and says the ledger keeps every revision (new projects only: it is a seed).
- **`_message_before_update`** tells every project updating from v0.2.0 or earlier that sections worked before the update carry no revision record, that `make compare` shows each with the stages its entry holds, and that nothing is migrated.
- **`DESIGN.md`** adds D45 and extends D12 and D38, with the matching rows of Sections 3.2, 4.3, 5, 6 and 7.
- **`VERSION`** is 0.3.0.

### Fixed

- **A local workflow overrides a template workflow by slug, whatever its number, as D26 says.** `run-workflow` step 3, `.claude/rules/syntek-author/02-skills.md`, `manuscript/workflows/CLAUDE.md` and `typeset/workflows/CLAUDE.md` and their `local/` pairs said a local folder overrode only a template folder of the same name, number included. The `local/` pairs are seeds, so their wording changes for new projects only.
- **Rule 01 now says the `CONTEXT.md` and `CLAUDE.md` in `handoffs/`, `learning/` and `assets/` are template-owned**; only what the author puts in those folders is author-owned, which is how `copier update` already treats them.
- **The `DATE` question accepted a day the calendar lacks**, such as 30/02/2027, which then made the worked example's revision marker fail `provenance.py check` on a project nobody had touched. The validator now refuses it, and `check` calls such a marker date 'a day the calendar lacks' instead of asking for the DD/MM/YYYY form it already has.
- **A ledger entry saved with a byte-order mark** (as some Windows editors save) was rejected as having no frontmatter and left out of `make provenance`. Entries, briefs and the register are now read with the mark set aside.
- **`adapt-section` wrote `pending` in an open row's decision cell**, a word D38 does not have, which `provenance.py` reported as an unknown decision. The cell now stays empty until the author decides, in the skill and in both adapt workflows (D38).
- **`make provenance UNIT=` took only the brief's filename stem**: given a path, it reported 'No promoted sections yet' for a unit that had them. `UNIT=` now also takes a path to the unit's folder or brief (`manuscript/src/03-the-ford`, `planning/src/units/03-the-ford.md`), read as its last part, as `make tex-check` already did; `make compare` reads it the same way.

### Migration

- **No migration ships, and none is needed.** Nothing moves (D44 asks for a migration only when a release moves author work), no seed's required content changes, and every addition to an entry is optional, so an entry written before the update renders with the stages it holds. Entries are never backfilled, because nobody can say afterwards who made each change. `make compare` shows an older AI-drafted section from its AI original to its final, every change unattributed and grey, and an older author-drafted one as its final alone, and says the stages were not recorded. Such an entry keeps its decision rows and Author final as before; a redraft starts a full record, and a section whose entry is first written after the update has one from the start. `_message_before_update` and `README.md` ('Updating a project') say the same.
- **Seeds you already have are not rewritten.** Your `README.md` does not gain the `make compare` line or its TeX packages (`make help` lists the target), and example ledger entries you kept, copied once, keep their v0.2.0 shape. `make compare` to PDF needs the TeX Live packages `xcolor` (latex-recommended), `ulem` (plain-generic), `paracol` (latex-extra) and `array`; `FORMAT=tex` needs Pandoc alone.

## [0.2.0] - 04/10/2026

Adopting v0.1.0 into the business repository the variant was extracted from showed that its business layout did not match how that repository works, that the template's rules read project values from places an adopted repository fills differently, and that two build targets could print or overwrite what they should not. This release answers each.

### Added

- **Business document families, chosen per project (D39).** A new multiple-choice question, `BUSINESS_FAMILIES`, offers `business` (proposals, statements of work, client guides and each client's facts; always included, and its validator refuses a list without it), `legal`, `email`, `accounting`, `social-media` and the opt-in `msp-scp`. Each chosen family ships its folder `library/src/<family>/` (with `templates/`, its client folders and `drafts/`), its standard `library/docs/reference/<family>-standards.md` (with any sub-document split from it), its `<family>-documents` skill and its create workflow, `library/workflows/10-create-a-business-document` to `15-create-an-msp-scp-document`. Each family left out ships none of them. Every family path is one `_exclude` line gated `DOC_TYPE == 'business' and '<family>' in BUSINESS_FAMILIES` (Section 3.5).
- **One project-owned settings file (D40).** `.claude/rules/syntek-author/00-project.md` is a new seed, written once from the answers and loaded at launch with the rules, which it outranks: `## Brief`, `## Paths` (with an 'Approvals' role), `## Memory headings`, `## Workflow aliases` and `## Overrides`. A template path with no role is moved by a redirect bullet under `## Overrides`, `` `<template path>` → `<project path>` ``, which every template file honours (rule 01 Section 9). Its build-side twin is the seed `tooling/project.mk` (D43). Both are in `_skip_if_exists`, anchored.
- **Additive adoption (D41).** `copier copy --trust --skip '*' --skip-tasks` adopts a repository by keeping every existing file and adding the template beside it. `adopt/adopt.sh --additive [--answers FILE]` (and the three `<kind>.sh` wrappers) previews that copy and never writes in the repository. It reports what the copy will add and keep (each kept file marked committed, staged, untracked or ignored, so a file left by an earlier attempt is seen before the copy keeps it as the repository's), each file of the repository's beside a same-named template file in a neighbouring folder with the `## Overrides` redirect that makes template files read it, a mode file that would sit inert beside a kept `SKILL.md`, each kept `CONTEXT.md` to extend by hand (and a kept `workflows/CONTEXT.md` without the routing table), two procedures that would share a workflow number, the build settings to set in `tooling/project.mk`, and a kept `Makefile` that would leave the template's targets uninstalled. On a business library it also names every template path the repository's ignore rules hide (with the rule), every kept file a family untick or option-off would later delete, each template signpost added beside the repository's own files, and a kept workflow that would sync the new `drafts/` folders (D37). Its first line names the source and ref it previewed, and its closing command copies from exactly that. `README.md` gives both adoption modes and when to choose each.
- **Project build settings and issuing (D43).** `tooling/project.mk` sets `BRAND_DIRS` (business: where `make flags` looks for brand files; default `standards/brand`), `LOGO_DIRS`, `DOCX_CONVERTER`, `FLAG_EXTRA_RE`, `ISSUE_STATUSES` and `MAINFONT` / `SANSFONT` / `MONOFONT`; the `Makefile` reads it with `-include`. `tooling/latex/symbol-fallback.tex` ships so a Markdown PDF keeps its symbol glyphs. `house-preamble.tex` adds `\houseclassification{<level>}`, which prints a document's classification in the header and footer of every page (`texcheck.py` reads it as layout), and `tooling/latex/skeleton.tex` gains the internal-note block, a version-history table and its family standard's disclaimer rule.
- **The six family skills (D39, Section 5.3).** `business-documents`, `legal-documents`, `email-documents`, `accounting-documents`, `social-media-documents` and `msp-scp-documents` hold each family's document types, required sections, house conventions and pre-issue checks, generalised from the source repository with every business and client detail removed. `msp-scp-documents` keeps its two required-sections sub-documents; the email and msp-scp standards each split one sub-document (`EMAIL-ANATOMY-AND-NAMING.md`, `MSP-SCP-POLICY-SUITE.md`) carrying the family's gate. The email family files by correspondent (`client-emails/`, `supplier-emails/`) rather than `client-docs/`. Each skill has its `Skill(<name>)` deny line in the root `.claude/settings.json` (dev isolation, Section 7).
- **Migrations (D44).** A root `migrations/` folder, with its pair, holds the scripts `copier update` runs when a release moves author work; `copier.yml`'s `_migrations` runs `migrations/v0.2.0-business-families.sh` on business projects crossing v0.2.0, and `migrations/v0.2.0-project-settings.sh` on every project crossing it. Each ends by printing the command that runs it again (a clone of the release, then the script), because the update's own copy of the template is gone by then.
- **Audits.** `generate-all.sh` adds business renders with every family (`msp-scp` included) and with `[business]` alone. New checks: `shipped-variants.sh` 10–11 (each family's skill, folder, standard and workflow ship exactly as chosen, through `fam-<family>` catalogue gates; no unknown or v0.1.0 family folder); `shipped-seeds.sh` 16–17 (`00-project.md` carries its five headings with no alias or override; `project.mk` sets only the D43 settings); `update-test.sh` 13–23 (a real update from the `v0.1.0` tag moves every business file byte for byte with nothing lost, carries each book's changed audience into `## Brief`, keeps a reader test re-answered during the update over an older hand edit and reports that edit as a conflict, and changes nothing on a second run; check 12 also requires the update message to name `BUSINESS_FAMILIES`); `tooling-smoke.sh` 21–32 (no git-ignored file or language folder read, the `ISSUE` guard and switches, `\fillme` counted, a file name git would quote still built, the classification on every page, `BRAND_DIRS` honoured); `adopt-test.sh` 11–24 (an additive copy changes no existing file, and its report names what each section promises, same-named files with their redirect and committed, untracked and ignored kept files included); and `doc-references.sh` check 4 reads a skill's whole description, folded lines included. `lexicon.py --self-test` also proves a language folder whose files git ignores is never found. CI's job [1/3] runs shellcheck (pinned through shellcheck-py) at warning level over `migrations/`, `adopt/`, `.github/scripts/` and the template's hooks; each audit names `# shellcheck source=SCRIPTDIR/_common.sh`, so no warning is disabled.

The seed-once example proposal moves to `library/src/business/drafts/example-proposal`; its brief and ledger entry keep their paths.

- **One rule for a recipient's recorded preference.** `standards/risk/BUSINESS.md` rule 3, the email standard and `email-documents` agree: it is recorded only on the author's instruction, as the preference alone, never with its reason or a diagnosis. An email carries no legal or financial disclaimer, and the Correspondence wording only where the disclaimers file gives one. `disclaimers.md` gains a `## Policy review-date notice` heading for the wording every policy carries, and a service level agreement follows the msp-scp family's sections where the project has that family.

Every Pandoc PDF takes the project's fonts and `symbol-fallback.tex`, `house-preamble.tex` inputs the same fallback, and every PDF build runs with `MKTEXTFM=0`. `SRC`, `OUT` and the book file list are rendered for books only.

### Fixed

- **`make flags` and `make lint` read git-ignored folders (D42)** — where credentials and local-only material live — and printed matching lines verbatim.
