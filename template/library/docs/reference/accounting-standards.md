# Accounting standards — invoices, reports and the figures in them

**Last Updated**: <%DATE%> **Version**: 0.1.0 **Maintained By**: <%AUTHOR_NAME%>
**Language**: British English (en_GB)

**What it is.** The standard for every document in `library/src/accounting/`: the documents that
state money. Forms (an invoice, a credit note, an expense report) are filled field by field from a
template; prose documents (a financial report, a budget's narrative) go through the section loop.
Either way, every figure traces to a source the author can name, and every total recalculates.

## Document types

| Type | Versioned | Where | Disclaimer class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invoice, credit note, receipt | no: the number is the record | `client-docs/<client-slug>/` | none |
| Expense report | no | the family root | none |
| Financial report | yes | the family root | financial documents |
| Budget or forecast | yes | the family root | financial documents |
| Pricing or costing model | yes, the previous one labelled superseded | the family root | none |
| Template | no | `templates/` | its documents' class |

## Invoice fields

Every invoice carries every one of these, none blank: the invoice number (`<YYYY>-<NNN>`, one
sequence per year across the family); the date; the due date, stated, from the business's standard
terms; the issuer's trading name, address and contact details; the bank details and the payment
reference to use; the client's legal or trading name and address from its `## Facts`; the line
items (Category · Description · Hours or quantity · Price); the sub-total; the tax line, as an
amount or as the statement that none is charged; the total. The business's own details and bank
details live only in the invoice template, entered by the author.

## Financial reports, budgets and forecasts

- **Document Control:** Title, Period, Version, Date produced, Owner.
- **Parts:** the disclaimer; a summary; the period's figures in tables (revenue, costs, margins);
  notes to the figures (anomalies, one-offs, the source of each figure); key takeaways. A forecast
  states its assumptions and labels every figure as an estimate.

## Names and review

| Type | Pattern | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice | `invoice-<YYYY>-<NNN>.tex` | none: a mistake is corrected by a credit note |
| Credit note, receipt | `credit-note-<YYYY>-<NNN>.tex`, `receipt-<YYYY>-<NNN>.tex` | none |
| Report, budget, forecast | `<doc-type>-<period>-v<major>-<minor>-<DD-MM-YYYY>.tex` | per period |
| Pricing model | `pricing-<description>-v<major>-<minor>-<DD-MM-YYYY>.md` | on any change of price |
| Expense report | `expense-report-<period>.tex` | none |

## How we apply it here

- Amounts carry two decimal places and the currency `00-project.md` `## Brief` sets, in the style
  sheet's format: the sign with no space.
- Time worked is hours and minutes, never decimal hours; where hours multiply a rate, the decimal
  follows in brackets on a part-hour line, so the client can check the arithmetic.
- Pricing data states whether it includes tax and carries a date.
- Statements, ledgers and supplier receipts holding bank or personal details stay out of the
  repository, or in a folder git ignores; no tool here reads an ignored folder.
- A table generated by a script is regenerated from the script, never edited by hand.

## Who implements it

- **Skills:** `accounting-documents` holds the types, fields and checks; `fact-check` traces every
  figure; `build` renders and reads the proof.
- **Workflows:** `library/workflows/13-create-an-accounting-document/`.

## Governing standard

`00-project.md` `## Brief` names the currency; `standards/style/style-sheet.md` owns currency,
number and date formats; `standards/risk/BUSINESS.md` owns what may never be written down; the
disclaimer wording lives in the disclaimers file `00-project.md ## Paths` names. This guide owns
the accounting family's types, fields, parts, names and reviews.
