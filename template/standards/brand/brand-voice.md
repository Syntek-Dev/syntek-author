The voice standard for every document the business issues: proposals, letters, emails, policies,
web and social copy, and the running text of instruments. `tone` enforces it at line edit (gate
V6.1); `draft-section` writes from it; `improve-section` cites it when it proposes a change. The
house drafting principles it builds on, the marks of running copy among them, are in
`standards/method/BUSINESS.md`, which template updates keep current; this file records what is
particular to this business. The person the business writes in is in
`standards/style/voice-notes.md`; the trading name, exactly as printed, is in `00-project.md`
`## Brief`.

**How to add an entry.** Under the section's heading, one bullet per decision:
`- **DD/MM/YYYY** — **<the decision>.** <the reason>`, and, for a mark, a real before and after
from the business's own documents. Remove the section's flag once its decision is made, record
the decision in `.claude/MEMORY.md` `## Decisions` (mapped in `00-project.md`
`## Memory headings`), and mark an overturned entry `*(Superseded DD/MM/YYYY — see below.)*`
rather than deleting it.

## 2. Two registers

**Requirement.** **Running copy** (proposals, letters, articles, the body of an email) uses full
sentences, has room to explain and never room to pad. **Microcopy** (subject lines, labels,
buttons, short notices) is terse and action-first, with no exclamation marks. Legal instruments
and policies keep a formal register; the brand voice reaches into them only as far as the entry
here records, and until there is one, not at all.

## 4. Concision is the default, and it has a boundary

**Requirement.** Answer the questions the reader would have asked anyway, in the fewest words that
still answer them. Cut first: a softener, a sentence restating an attachment, a trailing
reassurance, the second and third worked example. What must survive: the bolded lead-ins, the
anticipated answers, and every obligation (`standards/method/BUSINESS.md` rule 2).

_No entries yet._

**Why this rule exists.** Concise is not thin. A reader thanks a short document only if it still
answers their question.

## 6. What a copy review keeps, and what it tidies

**Requirement.** A copy review **keeps** directness, checkable claims, idiom, calm, an honest
limit and the next step. It **tidies** a superlative into the number behind it, an abstraction
into the concrete thing, passive into active, filler into nothing, en_US into en_GB, and an
exclamation mark into a full stop. It reports by location and applies only what the author
accepts.

_No entries yet._

**Why this rule exists.** A review that cannot say what it will leave alone sands every document
down to the same flat voice.

## 7. The highest-stakes copy

**Requirement.** Four kinds of copy get the most care:

- **Claims and guarantees**: quantified or qualified, never bare (`standards/risk/BUSINESS.md`
  rule 5).
- **Pricing**: exact, complete, with no hidden asterisk; every figure traced
  (`standards/method/BUSINESS.md` rule 7).
- **Errors and apologies**: never blame the reader; say what happened and what happens next.
- **Legal-adjacent copy**: sober, and deferring to the instrument it summarises.

**Why this rule exists.** These are the sentences a client quotes back when something goes wrong.
