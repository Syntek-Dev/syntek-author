# sensitive-content.md — handling the work's hardest subjects

**Last Updated**: <%DATE%> **Version**: 0.1.0 **Maintained By**: <%AUTHOR_NAME%>
**Language**: British English (en_GB)

The governing standard for the subjects this work handles that could wound a reader, endanger a
third party or cost the author: abuse, addiction, self-harm, violence, trauma, or whatever this
project's own list holds. **The subjects themselves are recorded once, in `.claude/MEMORY.md`
`## Sensitivities`, with the project's highest-risk class of claim; this file never names them**,
so a change of scope is one edit. The `sensitivity-pass` skill applies this standard as a
checklist before any unit touching those subjects is called done; first-person material is
handled through `research/src/testimony/` and the research workflow
`research/workflows/05-handle-testimony-safely/`. It adds to `risk.md`, never relaxes it.

Dates DD/MM/YYYY.

## 1. Handle hard material with care

**Requirement.** A sensitive subject is named plainly, without sensationalism, euphemism or
shame. The governing test for every passage is that it **names a hard truth with care**, never
that it **feels like an attack** on a reader who has lived it. Write so that a reader affected
by the subject is left able to seek help, not driven to hide.

> **Wrong:** a vivid, lingering account written to shock.
>
> **Right:** the fact stated plainly, its weight acknowledged, and a way forward in view.

**Why this rule exists.** Some readers of this work have lived what it describes. Shame drives
people into hiding and silence; a passage that leaves them able to speak has done its work.

---

## 2. Demand more of the hardest claims

**Requirement.** A claim about a sensitive subject meets `standards/method/method.md` and more:
every statistic names its study, year, population and jurisdiction; sub-populations and
jurisdictions are never blended; a dated or weak figure is stated with its caveat or cut. **Cite
what is defensible, not what is alarming.** The project's highest-risk class of claim, recorded
in `.claude/MEMORY.md` `## Sensitivities`, is never stated without a `verified` verdict.

**Why this rule exists.** A sensational figure that collapses under scrutiny discredits the
whole work on the subject where it most needed to be trusted, and hands its critics an excuse to
dismiss the harm itself.

---

## 3. Signpost help in plain prose

**Requirement.** At the close of a passage that may stir a reader, the work points to help in a
brief, plain-prose note tailored to who is likely to be reading. Every organisation, service or
contact named is verified against its own published source and dated, stays flagged `VERIFY`
until it is, and is checked again before every export. Nothing is invented, and no contact is
recalled from memory.

**Why this rule exists.** A reader stirred by a passage needs somewhere to go next; a wrong
number or a closed service in print fails the reader at the moment they reach for help.

---

## 4. Safeguarding, consent and the author's wellbeing

**Requirement.**

- **No identifying detail** of a child or a third party appears in the work; people are
  anonymised by default unless they have explicitly cleared otherwise.
- **Consent is load-bearing:** explicit, considered, recorded and never inferred (`risk.md`
  rule 3). Nothing from first-person testimony reaches the work verbatim without the author's
  explicit, considered decision.
- **First-person material is shaped with the author, never pasted and never rewritten
  silently**: a skill proposes, and the author's words stay the author's.
- **Pause, flag and defer:** on a hard passage, stop and ask rather than push through; flag a
  safeguarding or legal risk for the author's decision.
- **Never fabricate or embellish personal detail**, and never overwrite a draft of this material
  without confirmation.

**Why this rule exists.** Safeguarding is a legal duty, consent cannot be withdrawn once printed,
and an author writing their own hard story must be able to stop. Nothing is called done that the
author has not chosen.
