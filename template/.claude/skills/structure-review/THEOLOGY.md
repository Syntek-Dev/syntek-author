# THEOLOGY.md — structural review of a theology book

A theology book is reviewed as an argument, as a book a publisher must want, and as counsel a
church will act on. Six lenses read it, each alone. The panel reports how each lens reads the work;
it never settles a theological question, and it never replaces the four structural gates that run
after it.

## Paths and unit

- **The unit** is a chapter: `manuscript/src/NN-kebab-title/NN-kebab-title.md`, with its brief in
  `planning/src/units/` and its argument map in `planning/src/arguments/`.
- **One chapter:** step 4 of `manuscript/workflows/05-review-a-chapter/`. After this review the
  workflow runs the sub-gates V4.1 to V4.4 in `standards/verification/THEOLOGY.md`
  (`argument-audit`, `category-check`, `tradition-check`, `steelman`); this review may point at
  where they should look, but never stands in for them.
- **The whole book, or a part:** `planning/workflows/09-review-the-whole-work/`.
- **Extra reads:** `standards/method/THEOLOGY.md`; `standards/risk/THEOLOGY.md`;
  `manuscript/docs/reference/main-text-and-footnotes.md`; the maps in
  `research/src/contested-readings/` the scope leans on.

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 2 — also read:** each chapter's argument map; the contested-reading maps it relies on; in
  `.claude/MEMORY.md`, any objection the author has designated as standing and any declared bias
  (`standards/method/THEOLOGY.md` rules 7 and 8), which the review must not 'fix'.
- **Step 4 — the panel, in this order:**
  1. *Thesis critic* — does the chapter advance the thesis in `.claude/CLAUDE.md` Section 1; where
     does a conclusion outrun its evidence; what is the hinge the argument turns on, and is it
     argued or assumed?
  2. *Acquisitions editor* — is there a book here a publisher could sell; is the chapter's promise
     clear on its first page; would this chapter be missed if cut? Market claims and comparable
     titles are claims to verify.
  3. *Practical theologian* — does the theology reach practice honestly; is the application earned
     by the argument or attached to it; is pastoral application kept distinct from exegesis?
  4. *Practitioner* — would a minister or a church leader find this workable in a real
     congregation; what would they push back on; does the chapter examine a practice without
     despising those who keep it (`standards/risk/THEOLOGY.md` rule 1)?
  5. *Book architect* — is the chapter in the right place in the outline; does it carry its weight;
     what does it set up and pay off across chapters; where does it overlap another; is the split
     between main text and footnotes right?
  6. *Risk* — `standards/risk/THEOLOGY.md`: traditions described as their holders would recognise
     them; no pastoral confidence; children and the vulnerable protected; hard counsel with a route
     to help; the sensitive-content standard as well, where the project has one.
- **Step 5 — also:** the four things never demoted to a footnote (the concession, the contested
  reading, the author's own stake, the category shift): any of them found only in a note is a
  structural finding.
- **Step 6 — also:** next steps may name `planning/workflows/02-map-the-argument/` for a re-mapped
  argument, and `manuscript/workflows/10-steelman-the-objections/` for an objection that needs work.
- **Whole-book scope — also:** does the thesis progress from chapter to chapter, or is one claim
  argued twice; do the verdicts of two chapters contradict each other; does the book end where its
  opening promised; where the project has a book proposal, does the manuscript deliver what it
  pitches?

## Domain rules

- **The panel reads; it does not re-argue.** A lens may say an argument is weak and why; it never
  substitutes its own theology for the author's.
- **A standing objection is the author's choice** and is not a finding (`standards/method/THEOLOGY.md`
  rule 7); a declared bias is not a defect to neutralise (rule 8).
- **Contested readings stay contested.** A lens that prefers one reading says so as a lens, under
  the dissent if others differ, never as the review's verdict.

## Examples

> **Verdict line (thesis critic, significant).** `the-turn`, paragraphs 3 to 5: the conclusion that
> a welcome is finished only when the guest is expected back rests on one example; the argument
> map lists two. The second example is missing from the prose.

> **Open decision 2.** The acquisitions editor and the book architect disagree about whether this
> chapter should open the book. Options: keep it second (architect), or move it first (editor).
> Recommendation: keep it second, because chapter 1 sets up the vocabulary this chapter assumes.
> Both views are kept under the honest dissent.

> **Claim to verify.** 'Books on church hospitality sell mainly to clergy.' Asserted by the
> acquisitions-editor lens from no source; flagged `VERIFY` for `fact-check`.
