# THEOLOGY.md — prototype, theology mode

The two branches for a theology book, ARGUMENT and VOICE, and what each relaxes.

## Paths and unit

- **Where a spike lives:** `manuscript/src/NN-kebab-title/drafts/SPIKE-<slug>.md`, beside the
  chapter's section drafts; a spike spanning chapters sits in the drafts folder of the chapter
  its question most concerns.
- **Reads (step 1):** the brief's `## Claims and categories`, the argument map in
  `planning/src/arguments/`, any contested-reading map in `research/src/contested-readings/`.
- **The unit's guide:** `manuscript/docs/reference/section-anatomy.md`.

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 2 — also name the branch: ARGUMENT or VOICE.** ARGUMENT asks 'does this argument survive
  being written out?'; VOICE asks 'how should this sound?'
- **Step 4 — also, ARGUMENT:** draft the chapter's spine at rough full length: the thesis in one
  sentence, the concession at full strength, the argument, and the conclusion with its claim
  category. The hardest paragraph is usually the concession, or the point where a contested
  reading has to be named in the body; write it first.
- **Step 4 — also, VOICE:** vary what genuinely differs: the register (main text against the
  depth that belongs in a footnote), how much the author tells on themselves, how early a
  disclosure lands, whether the passage opens on a scene or on a question.
- **Step 4 — also, what relaxes:** looked-up citations (placeholders instead), the footnote
  apparatus, claim-category labels, the steelman and tradition checks, proofreading and the
  British-English sweep. **What does not relax:** confidentiality, and the rule that scripture is
  quoted only from the text: a verse not yet checked is a placeholder, never a recollection.
- **Step 5 — also** record an ARGUMENT verdict in the brief's `## Claims and categories` or the
  argument map; a VOICE verdict that should govern later chapters goes to the `Decisions` heading
  of `.claude/MEMORY.md` (mapped in `00-project.md` `## Memory headings`) through the gate, so
  later drafting stays consistent.

## Domain rules

- **The most useful thing a spike does here is find an argument that does not survive prose.**
  Conceding at full strength, naming the contested reading in the body and keeping each
  conclusion in its category are demanding; a plan can satisfy them on paper and be unwritable in
  practice. A spike that finds this before drafting saves a chapter.
- **Never take a spike's reading of a passage as settled.** If it depended on one, the reading
  goes to `research/workflows/03-map-a-contested-reading/`.
