# FICTION.md — approach-a-reader: literary agents

The fiction mode of `approach-a-reader`. The reader is a **literary agent**, approached with a
query: a letter, and whatever the agency's own guidelines ask to receive with it. `SKILL.md` owns
the procedure; this file owns the paths, the package, the guidelines and the no-chase rule.

## Paths and unit

- **The unit** is one tailored query to one named agent.
- **The tracker:** `proposal/src/submissions/tracker.md`, with the columns Agent · Agency · Why
  them · Their guidelines · Sent · Status · Reply · Requested · Next action · Due, and the statuses
  `to query` · `queried` · `requested` · `passed` · `offer` · `withdrawn` · `lapsed`.
- **Drafts:** `proposal/src/submissions/drafts/<agent-slug>.md`; the reader's slug is the agent's.
- **The master text:** `proposal/src/query-letter.md`, which stays general; the tailored copy lives
  only in the draft.
- **The rest of the package:** `proposal/src/synopsis-short.md`, `proposal/src/synopsis-long.md`,
  `proposal/src/comp-titles.md`, and the opening chapters through
  `proposal/src/sample/sample-index.md`.
- **Extra reads:** `proposal/docs/reference/query-package-anatomy.md`, and
  `standards/method/FICTION.md` rule 6 (the story bible is the source of truth the synopses match).

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 1 — also** note every row at `queried` or `requested`: simultaneous submissions are the
  norm unless an agency says otherwise, and the author must know who is still considering the book.
- **Step 2 — also** 'why them' means three things, each checked on the agency's own pages: they
  represent this genre, they are open to submissions, and their list has room for this book. Name a
  book on their list or a wish they have published. If there is no reason, do not query.
- **Step 3 — also** the agency's guidelines win over every target in the package: record exactly
  what they want (letter, synopsis length, how many opening chapters or words, file format, how to
  send) in *Their guidelines* before drafting. For a first novel, query only a finished, revised
  manuscript; the sample is the opening chapters, consecutive and promoted.
- **Step 4 — also** tailor only the opening (why this agent) and whatever their guidelines change;
  the hook, the story and the biography stay as the author approved them in the master.
- **Step 5 — also** about a page: the personal line first; the hook, the protagonist, the stakes
  and the choice; title, genre and word count to the nearest thousand; two or three comparable
  titles from `proposal/src/comp-titles.md`; a short biography. A query letter's form rarely has
  room for an exit line; follow the agency's form.
- **Step 6 — also** never claim a comparable title's sales, another agent's interest, or an offer
  that has not been made; the synopsis attached tells the whole story, ending included, and agrees
  with the story bible.
- **Step 10 — also** *Sent* is the date the author sent it, and the row moves to `queried`. A
  request records what was asked for (a partial or the full manuscript) and when it went; a pass
  records any reason verbatim; an offer is reported to the author at once.
- **Step 11 — also** **never chase a query.** Once the agency's stated response time passes, mark
  the row `lapsed` (where no time is stated, the author sets one). A requested manuscript may be
  chased once, politely, after the time the agent gave.

## Domain rules

- **The agency's guidelines win.** Send exactly what is asked, in the form asked; a package that
  ignores them is often never read.
- **The synopsis is not a teaser.** It tells the whole plot, ending included, conventionally in the
  present tense and the third person, and it agrees with the manuscript and the story bible.
- **An offer changes everyone's position.** When one comes, the author tells every agent still
  considering the book; that is the convention, and it is fair.
- **Personalise in the draft, never the master.** A master that drifts towards one agent stops
  being a master.

## Examples

Invented agents, agencies and books, for shape only.

> **Personal line:** 'You have said you are looking for fantasy rooted in a working landscape, and
> *The Saltmarsh Year* on your list is why I thought of you first.'

> **Book details:** 'THE FORD is a fantasy novel of 98,000 words, for readers of [comparable title]
> and [comparable title].'

> **Tracker row, after a request:** `| Ada Quill | [agency] | Represents rural fantasy; open to
> submissions | Letter, one-page synopsis, first three chapters | 02/12/2026 | requested | Asked
> for the full manuscript | Full, sent 09/12/2026 | Await reply | 09/02/2027 |`
