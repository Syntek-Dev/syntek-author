# THEOLOGY.md — spelling in a theology book

A theology book carries words no general dictionary settles: the names of biblical books, people
and places, transliterated Hebrew and Greek, and the terms of particular traditions. It also quotes
texts whose spelling is not the book's to change.

## Paths and unit

- **The unit** is a chapter: `manuscript/src/NN-kebab-title/NN-kebab-title.md`, with section drafts
  in its `drafts/` folder. Main text and footnotes are both checked.
- **The pass** is step 10 of `manuscript/workflows/05-review-a-chapter/`.
- **Extra reads:** the default translation in `00-project.md` `## Brief`, and how Scripture is
  quoted in the Project settings of `standards/style/style-sheet.md`; any transliteration scheme
  the style sheet records; the contested terms in `standards/style/terminology.md`.
- **Where corrections go:** an accepted spelling correction may be applied in the chapter file
  directly and recorded in that section's ledger entry
  (`manuscript/workflows/05-review-a-chapter/`, 'Applying agreed fixes'); in a draft, in the draft
  (step 7).

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 2 — also add to the known words:** the names of the books of the Bible and any house
  abbreviations for them; people and places as the default translation spells them; transliterated
  words as the style sheet transliterates them; the terms in `standards/style/terminology.md`,
  including the names of traditions and their offices.
- **Step 3 — also:** a Scripture quotation keeps the translation's spelling exactly, US spellings
  and archaic forms included, and is never corrected; a quotation that seems not to match its
  translation goes to `fact-check`. Capitals for divine names and pronouns follow the style sheet in
  the author's prose and the translation inside a quotation.
- **Step 3 — also:** the singular and plural of book names ('a psalm', 'the Psalms') and their
  abbreviations are used as the style sheet settles; where it is silent, report the first two
  conflicting uses and propose an entry.
- **Step 4 — also:** biblical names are near-misses of each other more often than of English words
  ('Philippians' and 'Philemon' are both books); report the near-miss against the book the
  reference actually points to.
- **Step 5 — also:** a transliteration with or without diacritics, and an older translation's
  spelling inside a quotation, are deliberate when the style sheet records them.

## Domain rules

- **Quoted Scripture and quoted writers keep their source's spelling.** The book's own prose follows
  en_GB and the style sheet.
- **One transliteration scheme.** If the style sheet records none, propose one on the first
  transliterated word, rather than correcting each instance a different way.
- **A term from one tradition** is spelt and capitalised as that tradition does when the book is
  describing it, and as the style sheet settles in the author's own voice.
- **Footnotes are the specialist's register,** not a lower standard: they are checked line by line
  like the main text.

## Examples

> **'Phillipians' → 'Philippians'.** Three places: `opening` line 4; `the-turn` lines 2 and 18.

> **Not reported.** A US spelling inside a quotation from a translation that uses it.

> **'judgement' and 'judgment'.** Both appear in the author's prose (four and two places, listed).
> Proposed style-sheet entry for approval: '"judgement" in the author's prose; quoted text keeps its
> source's form.'

> **Question.** 'Shema' appears with and without a capital in the author's prose. Is the lower-case
> form deliberate? If not, which form should the style sheet record?
