# FICTION.md — improve-section, fiction mode

The domain for proposing improvements to a beat of a novel that the author wrote: what a
structural pass checks, which oddities are voice, and what stays out of the diff.

## Paths and unit

- **Unit:** a chapter. **Section:** one scene beat.
- **Procedure:** `manuscript/workflows/03-improve-your-draft/`.
- **Draft:** `manuscript/src/NN-kebab-title/drafts/<NN>-<section-slug>.md`; its brief is
  `planning/src/units/NN-kebab-title.md`.
- **Registers:** the narrator's voice in `standards/style/voice-notes.md`, and each character's
  voice markers in their file in `world/src/characters/`.
- **Guide:** `manuscript/docs/reference/scene-craft.md`. **Method:** `standards/method/FICTION.md`.

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 3 — also read the beat's people and causes:** the file of every character who speaks,
  the beat's entry in `planning/src/causality.md`, and the brief's `## Continuity facts`.
- **Step 4 — also check the beat's engine.** At `rework`, check that the beat has a goal, a
  conflict and an outcome; that it holds one point of view; that each turn follows from a cause
  ('because' and 'therefore', never 'and then'); and that scene and sequel alternate as the
  chapter needs. A problem with what happens is a question for the author, not a proposal. At
  `edit`, these are notes.
- **Step 5 — also keep each voice its own.** Dialogue keeps its speaker's voice markers; a
  proposal that makes two characters sound alike is not an improvement. Dialect spellings,
  deliberate fragments and a character's verbal habits are voice, not slips. Dialogue punctuation
  follows the style sheet.
- **Step 7 — also keep out of the lane:** an event, a cause, a continuity fact, a name, a
  constructed word or its span, and anything the point-of-view character knows. Each concern about
  these is a question, and a contradiction with the story bible is reported with both locations.

## Domain rules

- **Point-of-view discipline** (`standards/method/FICTION.md` Section 5): a line proposal never
  lets the narration know more than its point-of-view character.
- **Every beat keeps its cause** (Section 1): tightening that removes a cause is a structural
  change, raised as a question.
- **The story bible is the source of truth** (Section 6): a proposal never changes a fact to suit
  a sentence.
- **Restraint with constructed words**: a proposal never adds one, and never glosses one the
  author left unglossed without asking.

## Examples

An invented proposal and an invented note at `edit` strength:

```text
2. Paragraph 1, lines 2–3 · edit
   Before: 'She was cold. She was cold and she was afraid of the water.'
   After:  'She was cold, and afraid of the water.'
   Reason: the repetition reads as accidental here; it is not one of the narrator's marks.

Note (structural, not proposed at edit): the beat's outcome arrives in the last line before its
conflict has peaked; a rework would hold it back one paragraph.
```
