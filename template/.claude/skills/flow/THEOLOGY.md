# THEOLOGY.md — flow in a theology book

A theology book is an argument told in one voice across chapters written out of order. Its flow is
the argument's flow: each section picks up the step the last one established, the threads the
book carries survive every seam, and the move from text to conclusion stays visible however
smoothly the prose runs.

## Paths and unit

- **The unit** is a chapter: `manuscript/src/NN-kebab-title/NN-kebab-title.md`, its sections between
  `<!-- section: <slug> -->` markers.
- **The pass** is step 8 of `manuscript/workflows/05-review-a-chapter/`.
- **Extra reads:** `planning/src/outline.md` and the chapter's brief (the threads the book carries,
  and this chapter's place in them); the chapter's argument map in `planning/src/arguments/`;
  `manuscript/docs/reference/main-text-and-footnotes.md` (the two registers).

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 1 — also:** list the threads the outline and the brief say this chapter carries forward
  (a question opened earlier, an image, a promise to the reader), so a seam that drops one can be
  seen.
- **Step 2 — also:** at each seam, the next section picks up the claim the last one established; a
  section that starts the argument afresh, as if the reader had not read the one before, is a
  finding.
- **Step 5 — also:** the same verse quoted twice in one chapter (a refrain, or an accident? ask); a
  contested term used in two senses (`standards/style/terminology.md` records the reading adopted).
- **Step 6 — also:** any convention the voice notes record about tense or person (for example, the
  author's own story told in the past tense) is held in every section; the register shifts at the
  footnote line, not in the middle of a body paragraph.
- **Step 7 — also:** a concession placed after its response is `steelman`'s to judge; a seam that
  hides a move between claim categories is `category-check`'s. Name the place and hand it on.

## Domain rules

- **Hedges and category signals are not repetition.** 'On this reading' three times in a section
  may be the argument being honest; never suggest trimming one for rhythm.
- **A concession's position is not a matter of rhythm.** Never suggest moving one after the
  response to it.
- **A whole-work reading follows the threads across chapters,** in the outline's order.

## Examples

> **Blocking.** Seam between `opening` and `the-turn`: `the-turn` opens 'Churches have long asked
> whether…', restating the claim `opening` has just made. Suggestion: open on what the claim asks
> of a church.

> **Friction.** `opening` lines 4 and 21: the same visitor anecdote frames two paragraphs.
> Accident, or refrain? If an accident, keep the first.

> **Handed on.** `the-turn` line 30: the strongest objection is answered before it is conceded. For
> `steelman`.
