# FICTION.md — sensitivity-pass: depiction, real people and real peoples

The fiction mode of `sensitivity-pass`. Invention does not exempt a story from harm: it can defame,
endanger or exploit as surely as an essay, and a people or a language modelled on a real one can
carry a stereotype back to real people. `SKILL.md` owns the procedure; this file owns the paths and
the depiction rules the four checks apply here.

## Paths and unit

- **The unit** is a chapter: `manuscript/src/NN-kebab-title/NN-kebab-title.md`, or a section draft
  in its `drafts/` folder, or one testimony note in `research/src/testimony/` recording real events
  behind the story.
- **Extra reads:** `standards/risk/FICTION.md` (rules 1 to 4 and 6 bear on this pass); the files in
  `world/src/characters/` and `world/src/places/` for everyone and everywhere in scope;
  `world/src/names-register.md`; the community research in `research/src/setting/`.
- **Where the project builds peoples, cultures and languages,** also read the files for each one
  the scope draws on: a culture's depiction-check internal note, and each language's recorded
  real-world models and deliberate echoes.
- **Decisions** that bind this pass live under the `Sensitivities` heading of `.claude/MEMORY.md`
  (mapped in `00-project.md` `## Memory headings`): the subjects
  that need a content note, whether and where content notes appear, and whether a sensitivity
  reader is engaged.

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 1 — also** read `standards/risk/FICTION.md` and the decisions above; an undecided
  content-note or sensitivity-reader question is reported as `AUTHOR TO CONFIRM`, never assumed.
- **Step 4 — also** real events from the author's life or other people's are transformed until
  the people involved could not be identified by those who know them, unless they gave explicit,
  recorded consent (risk rule 6).
- **Step 5 — also** depiction is a choice made knowingly (risk rule 1): flag harm shown for shock
  rather than for the story, and a scene that needs a content note the recorded decision does not
  give it. Self-harm and suicide carry no method detail, are never presented as a solution or as
  romantic, and are checked against the recognised safe-messaging guidance for the jurisdiction of
  publication, cited and dated, with `VERIFY` until checked (risk rule 2).
- **Step 6 — also** real-world detail about harm (medicine, law, addiction, weapons) is a checkable
  claim for `fact-check`. A character from a community, culture, faith or condition the author does
  not share is researched from that community's own published voices, recorded in
  `research/src/setting/`; dialect, customs and slurs a character uses are never invented
  (risk rule 4).
- **Step 7 — also** a novel's help note usually sits in the front or back matter rather than at
  the scene; where it goes is the author's recorded decision, and every service it names is
  verified and dated like any other.
- **Step 8 — also** check **real people and places** (risk rule 3): no real living person does or
  says what they did not publicly do or say; an invented figure does not resemble a real one by
  name, office or circumstance; no real, identifiable business, institution or official is shown
  in a damaging light.
- **Step 8 — also** check **real peoples behind invented ones**: no real people reduced to one
  trait, no sacred practice used as decoration, and the borrowing recorded in the culture's
  depiction-check note.
- **Step 8 — also** check **real languages behind invented ones**, wherever a language or its
  names are modelled on real ones. Borrowing the sound, rhythm and naming habits of a living,
  minority, Indigenous or sacred language is acceptable; lifting its words or sacred terms, or
  caricaturing it, is flagged. A hostile or villainous people whose language or names are modelled
  on a real ethnic group's language is **always flagged**, because readers carry the association
  back to real people (harsh-consonant stereotypes are the usual trap); suggest an ancient or
  extinct model, or a blend. A prominent invented name or word that means something unintended in
  a model language or in English slang is flagged unless its record shows the false-friend check
  was run and any echo is deliberate, verified and sourced.

## Domain rules

- **Depicting harm is not endorsing it; handling it is the author's choice,** made once and
  recorded, so it does not drift scene by scene.
- **Readers who need a warning need it before the scene, not after.**
- **Representation is researched, not assumed,** and whether to engage a sensitivity reader is
  the author's decision.
- **Real-world models give structure and flavour, never words.** Every claim about a real language
  or people is researched and cited in `research/src/setting/`, never asserted from memory.
- **Report, never repair.** A depiction finding offers options; the scene is the author's, and is
  changed only through the section loop.

## Examples

Invented passages and peoples, for shape only.

> **Safeguarding, blocking:** the section `the-raid` shows the Vell, the story's raiders, speaking a
> language whose names and sounds follow one living language closely. Readers will hear a real
> people as the villains. Options: an extinct model, a blend of unrelated models, or a different
> people's language for the raiders.

> **Care, finding:** the section `the-river`, opening 'She thought about the water…' — the
> character's suicidal thoughts are described with method detail. Safe-messaging guidance (to be
> cited and dated) advises against it; the author decides how the scene keeps its weight without it.

> **Real people, finding:** an invented mayor of a real town shares the real office-holder's
> surname and term; change the name or the office, or record the author's reasoned decision.
