# Skill: Sensitivity pass (<%PROJECT_NAME%>)

Locale: en_GB · <%TIMEZONE%> · dates DD/MM/YYYY.

Some readers of this work have lived what it describes, and some of its material is someone's own
story. This skill applies the sensitive-content standard as a checklist: does each passage name a
hard truth with care, are the hardest claims sourced, does a stirred reader have somewhere to go,
and is everyone in it protected, the author included? You are a **reviewer, not a rewriter**:
report with exact locations and suggested fixes, **never rewrite first-person testimony**, and
never treat silence as consent.

## How to run the pass

1. **Read in before opening anything.** Read `standards/risk/sensitive-content.md`,
   `standards/risk/risk.md` and `.claude/MEMORY.md` `## Sensitivities` (the subjects, the
   highest-risk class of claim, consent decisions and hard lines). For testimony, also read
   `research/src/testimony/CONTEXT.md` and `CLAUDE.md` and `research/docs/reference/handling-testimony.md`.
   Then agree the scope with <%AUTHOR_FIRST_NAME%>: a testimony note, a section draft, or a unit.
   *Complete when:* every file above has been read and the scope is named; no note has been
   opened before this.

2. **Check the author's readiness.** Where the material is the author's own story, or one
   entrusted to them, confirm the author is willing to work on it now. The work is never done
   alone, and it never pretends to be the author's support; if there is any doubt, pause and say
   so. *Complete when:* the author has said to go on, or the pass is paused at their word.

3. **Read the whole once, gently, and keep the original.** Read the scope through before marking
   anything. Never overwrite a testimony note or a draft: any change is a dated addition, or a
   working copy saved beside it and plainly named. *Complete when:* the scope has been read whole,
   and the original is untouched.

4. **Hold every use of testimony to its consent.** For each passage that draws on a note in
   `research/src/testimony/`, check the note's frontmatter: `consent` allows this use
   (`shape-only` informs without identifying detail or wording; `paraphrase` retells in the
   author's words; `verbatim` quotes only the named passages); nothing in `hard_lines` is used;
   `consent_date` is filled; an entrusted account names its `teller` by a pseudonym only; the unit
   is listed in `serves`. A use beyond consent, or any use of a `not-asked` or `private` note, is
   blocking. Record consent only as the person's own answer, through the testimony procedure's
   step 7; never infer it from silence or an earlier yes. *Complete when:* every testimony-derived
   passage is marked within consent or blocking, with the note and field cited.

5. **Check 1, care.** Test every stirring passage against the governing test: it **names a hard
   truth with care**, never **feels like an attack** on a reader who has lived it. Flag
   sensationalism, euphemism, shame, disclosure for its own sake, and any child treated as a case
   study rather than someone to protect. In first-person testimony, flag and do not rewrite: the
   words are shaped with the author, in their voice, never over it. *Complete when:* every stirring
   passage is marked passed or carries a finding with its location.

6. **Check 2, the hardest claims.** Every statistic names its study, year, population and
   jurisdiction; sub-populations and jurisdictions are never blended; a dated or weak figure
   carries its caveat or is cut. A claim in the highest-risk class recorded in `.claude/MEMORY.md`
   needs a `verified` verdict in `research/src/evidence/`. Cite what is defensible, not what is
   alarming. A gap goes to `fact-check` and carries `<!-- VERIFY: … -->`; testimony is never
   evidence for a claim about others. *Complete when:* every claim in scope has its verdict, or a
   flag and a route.

7. **Check 3, signposting.** At the close of a passage that may stir a reader (or the author), a
   brief, plain-prose note points to help, tailored to who is likely to be reading. Every
   organisation, service or contact named is verified against its own published source and dated,
   carries `VERIFY` until it is, and is checked again before every export. Never name a source of
   help from memory. *Complete when:* every stirring passage has its signpost, or the missing one
   is reported with the place it belongs.

8. **Check 4, safeguarding and the author's wellbeing.** No identifying detail of a child or a
   third party, remembering that a person can be identified by role, place or timing as well as
   name; people are anonymised by default unless they explicitly cleared a detail. A safeguarding
   or legal risk is flagged with `<!-- AUTHOR TO CONFIRM: … -->`, stating the risk and the options,
   and never decided here. The author's first-person voice is kept; nothing personal is fabricated
   or embellished; on a hard passage, pause and ask. *Complete when:* every person in scope is
   protected or flagged, and every risk is with the author.

9. **Report, and hand back only what the author chooses.** Deliver the report below. Record a
   decision in `.claude/MEMORY.md` `## Sensitivities`, dated, only when the author makes it. In the
   testimony procedure, name the passages that may move towards a chapter only on the author's
   word and within recorded consent. Nothing is called done that the author has not chosen.
   *Complete when:* the report is delivered, every decision taken is dated, and what waits on the
   author is listed.

## The report

A prioritised list, **blocking first**. For each item:

- **Check** — consent · care · the hardest claims · signposting · safeguarding.
- **Location** — the file, the section marker or note heading, and the opening words.
- **Why** — the rule it meets or misses, in a sentence.
- **Fix** — a concrete suggestion for the author's own prose; for first-person testimony, the
  concern only, for the author's decision, with no rewritten words.
- **Route** — for a sourcing gap, the verdict so far (in the vocabulary of
  `standards/method/method.md` rule 7) and who closes it: `fact-check`, `research`, or the author.

End by saying whether any fix would reopen a promoted section, and listing what waits on the
author.

## Anti-patterns

- **Rewriting testimony.** Smoothing someone's own words into publishable prose destroys what the
  material is for, and takes a decision that is theirs.
- **Inferring consent.** Silence, an old conversation or 'they would not mind' is not consent.
- **Naming help from memory.** A wrong number or a closed service in print fails the reader who
  needed it.
- **Choosing the alarming figure.** A sensational statistic that collapses under scrutiny
  discredits the whole work on the subject where it most needs trust.
- **Pushing through.** A hard passage is paused, not finished to schedule.
- **Recording the subjects in a skill or a guide.** They live once, in `.claude/MEMORY.md`
  `## Sensitivities`.
