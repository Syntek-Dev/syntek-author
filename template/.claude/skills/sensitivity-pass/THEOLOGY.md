# THEOLOGY.md — sensitivity-pass: pastoral care, confidence and the church

The theology mode of `sensitivity-pass`. A theology book's hardest material is often pastoral: sin,
grief, harm, abuse and doubt, written for readers who may be living them, and drawing on what the
author has seen in ministry. `SKILL.md` owns the procedure; this file owns the paths and the
pastoral rules the four checks apply here.

## Paths and unit

- **The unit** is a chapter: `manuscript/src/NN-kebab-title/NN-kebab-title.md`, or a section draft
  in its `drafts/` folder, or one testimony note in `research/src/testimony/`.
- **Extra reads:** `standards/risk/THEOLOGY.md` (rules 1, 3, 4 and 5 bear on this pass) and
  `manuscript/docs/reference/main-text-and-footnotes.md` (what may move to a footnote, and what may
  not).
- **Evidence** for the hardest claims lives in `research/src/evidence/`; a statistic about
  churches, clergy or congregations is checked there like any other.

## Additions to the steps

- **Step 1 — also** read `standards/risk/THEOLOGY.md`, and note whether the scope draws on
  anything learned in pastoral care, confession, a safeguarding disclosure or a small group.
- **Step 4 — also** treat anything learned in pastoral confidence as testimony that needs explicit,
  recorded consent from the person concerned, even anonymised (risk rule 3): members of a
  congregation recognise each other in details no outsider would notice. Without that consent, the
  illustration is invented and says so, or drawn from a published source.
- **Step 5 — also** apply the pastoral form of the test: the passage convicts without condemning,
  so guilt has somewhere to go and shame has nothing to hide in. Flag a reader's sin, grief or harm
  named at the reader rather than with them, and a permissive answer hedged where a restrictive
  one is stated plainly (risk rule 1).
- **Step 6 — also** a figure about clergy, congregations or denominations distinguishes clergy
  from members, one country's data from another's, and the year and method of each survey. Every
  Scripture quotation used of pain carries its reference and translation, checked, never quoted
  from memory.
- **Step 7 — also** hard counsel carries a route to help in the main text, not only a footnote:
  name in plain prose the kind of person or service to turn to (a pastor, a doctor, a counsellor,
  a safeguarding lead), and any named organisation verified and dated (risk rule 5).
- **Step 8 — also** no identifying detail of a child or an adult at risk, from the author's
  ministry or anywhere else; a safeguarding failure is discussed only from public sources (inquiry
  reports, published reviews), with their dates (risk rule 4).

## Domain rules

- **Examine the practice; never despise the practitioner.** A reader who does what the passage
  examines should recognise themselves and keep reading.
- **What is heard in confidence never enters the work** without the person's explicit, recorded
  consent, however well disguised.
- **Children and the vulnerable are protected, never illustrations.**
- **The depth may go below; the care stays in the body.** Clinical qualification and scholarly
  detail may move to a footnote; the plain naming of the hard thing and the route to help may not.
- **The author's own story is theirs.** Where the author writes from their own life or ministry,
  their voice is kept and their pace is respected; the pass flags, the author decides.
