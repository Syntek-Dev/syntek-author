# Comparable titles — the shelf, and the differentiator

**Last Updated**: <%DATE%> **Version**: 0.1.0 **Maintained By**: <%AUTHOR_NAME%>
**Language**: British English (en_GB)

**What it is.** How to build a list of comparable titles that a publisher or an agent trusts:
which shelves to search, what each entry carries, and why the differentiator is the whole point.
A comparable title is not a claim that the book is as good as another. It tells the reader which
shelf the book sits on, and who will buy it.

## Search by shelf

Editors and agents think in shelves. Search the shelf the book will be **sold from** first, even
where its argument or its story sits nearer another; then the nearest neighbours in subject or
form; then, for non-fiction, the wider conversation the book must show it has read. A comparable
title from a shelf the book will never sit on tells the reader the author does not know the market.

## What each entry carries

**Author · title · publisher · year · its positioning in one line · how this book differs · the
date it was checked.** The differentiator is the entry; everything else is bibliography.

- **Not a differentiator:** 'also about a family after a flood'.
- **A differentiator:** 'tells the same kind of story backwards, from the reunion, so the reader
  knows who survives and reads for how'.

## Be honest where a comparable title is strong

A list that finds no serious competitors is not impressive; it reads as a search that was not
done, and a reader who knows the field will supply the missing title. If something close exists,
name it and say what this book adds. A pitch that has clearly read its rivals persuades more than
one that pretends to have none.

## Currency and scale

Prefer books from the last three to five years; where an older book is genuinely the nearest
neighbour, say why it still stands. Avoid the decade's biggest sellers: comparing a new book to a
phenomenon reads as naivety rather than ambition. Re-check the list before every submission; a
title that has gone out of print, or a newer one that has taken the space, changes the pitch.

## How we apply it here

- **Verify everything at the source:** author, title, publisher, year, and that it is in print.
  Never from memory; all five are trivially checkable, and one wrong entry costs the list its
  credibility.
- **Never invent a sales figure.** If sales are not public, give the positioning instead.
- **The number** of titles is set by the package anatomy in `proposal/docs/reference/`; a long list
  reads as padding.
- **The list lives** in the comparable-titles file that `proposal/src/CONTEXT.md` names.
