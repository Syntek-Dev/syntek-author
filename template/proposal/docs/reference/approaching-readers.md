# Approaching readers — asking for a yes the book needs

**Last Updated**: <%DATE%> **Version**: 0.1.0 **Maintained By**: <%AUTHOR_NAME%>
**Language**: British English (en_GB)

**What it is.** How to ask someone for a yes the book needs before publication (an endorser's
name, an agent's request for the manuscript) so that saying yes is easy and declining is easy too.
Both matter: an approach that makes declining awkward gets no reply at all, which is worse than a
no. The package anatomy in `proposal/docs/reference/` says which kind of reader this project
approaches and what they expect.

## Reach, not prestige

The organising question is never 'who is impressive?' but **'whose yes unlocks which audience, and
which objection does it answer?'** Prefer someone who can judge the book's claims, or its genre,
over a bigger name who cannot. Sequence for momentum: an early yes makes every later approach
easier to write.

## The moves of a good approach

1. **Why them, specifically.** Name the work of theirs, or the books on their list, that bear on
   this one. Generic praise reads as a mail-merge and is the commonest reason a good reader
   declines.
2. **The book, briefly, leading with the hook.**
3. **The author's position, plainly, where it matters:** a stake in the subject is stated in one
   clause, neither buried nor performed.
4. **The ask, with the effort attached:** what you want, what will be sent, roughly how long it
   takes, and by when; or exactly what the reader's own guidelines ask for.
5. **An easy, sincere exit,** where the form allows one.
6. **Nothing else.** No attachments they did not ask for, no marketing copy.

## Before and after the draft

Before: read the tracker (never a second first approach), check what actually exists to send in
`manuscript/src/`, and read the reader's published requirements. After: hand the draft to the
author, who sends it; the tracker records an approach only once it has gone. Chasing follows the
rule the package anatomy sets, and then stops.

## How we apply it here

- **Never invent** an endorsement, an interest, an offer or a deadline. Each is checkable, and each
  ends a relationship.
- **Write to a named person,** never a department or a general inbox where a name is available.
- **Proofread before it goes:** `spelling` and `grammar`, as a supportive report; names, titles
  and affiliations checked against their own published pages.
- **Keep their words verbatim.** A reply, an endorsement or a request is recorded exactly as it
  came, never improved.
