## What they are

A small human people who have lived along the middle reaches of one river for as long as their stories reach.
They are set apart from their neighbours by where they live and what they do there, not by anything in their bodies.

## Body and speech

Human in every respect that bears on speech: lips, teeth, tongue, breath and hearing are those of any reader.
Nothing in their bodies rules a sound in or out, so every limit on their language comes from its history, not from their mouths.
This is written down rather than left blank so that nobody building their language has to guess.

## Lifespan and generations

Ordinary human lives: most reach fifty, and the eldest ford-keepers seventy.
A generation is about twenty-five years, four to a century, and the world history counts its changes against that pace.

## Numbers and spread

A few thousand, in hamlets of a few dozen to a few hundred at each crossing along the river.
No settlement is large enough to be called a town.

## Homelands and movements

They hold the river from the falls down to the marshes.
Their stories say they came down from the hills above the falls; that migration has no event file yet, and its date is undecided.

## Relations to other peoples

Hill-folk upstream trade worked stone for dried fish, and marsh-dwellers downstream trade reeds and eels.
Neither neighbour is described yet, and the example casts neither as an enemy.
